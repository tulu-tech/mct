## PART A — CRITICAL ASSESSMENT

### Why the earlier direction felt weak, flat, and generic

The previous homepage wasn't *wrong* — it was *under-committed*. "Tasteful but forgettable" is the most common failure mode for trust-oriented editorial sites, and this one hit nearly every sub-symptom. Diagnosing concretely:

**1. The palette was timid — one accent doing all the work.**
A single pine accent (#1F5C4D) on CTAs plus a clay eyebrow (#A8553B) means the entire page has exactly *two* moments of color, both tiny, both at the same saturation. There's no tonal system — no way for a section to feel different from the one above it, no quiet-to-emphatic gradient. The result is a page that is 95% beige-and-charcoal with two specks of color. That reads as "safe template," not "art-directed." Trust brands like Consumer Reports and NerdWallet don't win on *less* color; they win on a *disciplined system* of color where each hue has a job. We had hues but no jobs.

**2. Weak hierarchy — everything is "medium."**
Flatness almost always traces to insufficient contrast in *type scale and weight*, not color. If your H2 is 28px and your body is 17px, the jump is only 1.6×, and nothing on the page commands attention. Premium editorial layouts run display-to-body ratios of 3–5×, plus deliberate weight contrast (a 300-weight serif headline over 500-weight sans labels). Without that, the eye has no entry point and no rhythm — it just scans gray soup. The hero headline almost certainly wasn't *big enough* or *light enough* to feel like a magazine cover.

**3. Generic line icons — the "default Feather/Lucide" tax.**
A standard 2px single-weight line-icon set is the visual equivalent of a system font. It signals "we used the defaults." Every SaaS landing page and SEO blog uses the same stroke icons, so they actively pull the site *toward* the thin-blog territory we must avoid. Icons alone can't carry brand identity — they're too small and too commoditized.

**4. The hero was under-art-directed.**
A "decision board" of 7 cards with a connector line is a good *concept*, but as executed it was likely a flat grid of equal-weight cards — which reads as a navigation menu or a feature grid, not a hero. Seven equal things = no focal point = no drama. A hero needs *one* dominant idea and *supporting* texture, not seven peers. And a thin drawn connector between 7 boxes is fussy without being beautiful; it adds clutter, not meaning.

**5. No illustration language at all.**
This is the biggest gap. The site had icons (tiny) and presumably photos or empty space (generic), but no *mid-scale proprietary illustration system* — the thing that actually makes Wirecutter, Stripe, and premium magazines feel owned and memorable. Without a custom illustration family, the 10 section visuals can never feel like one brand. They'll each look borrowed.

**6. Monotonous section rhythm — every section the same shape.**
Eyebrow → headline → paragraph → grid of cards, repeated 12 times down the page, on alternating beige surfaces. Even with perfect spacing, that's a metronome. Premium pages *breathe*: a dense comparison block followed by a near-empty quiet statement, a full-bleed tinted band followed by a tight margin-constrained checklist. The old page had no dynamics — no loud and soft — so it felt like one long flat note.

**7. Surfaces too close in value.**
#FFFFFF / #F6F4EF / #EFEDE6 are three shades of "almost the same warm white." The contrast between them is so subtle that alternating sections barely register as different. The page looks like one uniform sheet. Alternating surfaces only create rhythm if you can *see* the alternation.

### How this version is materially better

This direction fixes flatness at the *system* level, not by adding decoration:

- **A real palette with roles** — surfaces that actually step in value, two restrained accents (an evolved deep pine + a refined terracotta) plus a calm amber for caution, each with a defined job. Color becomes a wayfinding and hierarchy tool, not garnish.
- **A genuine type ramp** with 4–5× display-to-body contrast and deliberate weight pairing, so every section has a clear entry point.
- **A proprietary illustration language** — the "two-weight line + soft tint-fill" system below — that makes all 10 visuals unmistakably one family and gives the site the owned, hand-made-but-precise feel that separates a respected guide from a content farm.
- **Custom-drawn icons** in the same DNA as the illustrations, retiring the default-stroke look.
- **A choreographed section rhythm** that alternates density and quiet, surface and full-bleed, so the page has dynamics.
- **One distinctive hero** with a true focal hierarchy instead of seven equal cards.

### Visual personality — name and character

**"The Quiet Field Guide"** — *the well-kept reference desk of a trusted, independent advisor who has done the legwork so you don't have to.*

Picture the desk of someone meticulous and calm: warm paper, precise hand-drawn diagrams, a fountain-pen line, marginalia, a few carefully chosen reference tabs, good light. It is *analytical but human*, *authoritative but never clinical*, *warm but never folksy*. It feels like the field guide you'd actually trust on a shelf — Audubon, not a brochure; a well-made almanac, not a flyer.

This character does three jobs at once:
- **Editorial trust** comes from the paper-and-ink restraint, generous margins, serif authority, and diagrammatic clarity. It looks *considered*.
- **Visual richness** comes from the illustration family, the tonal surface stepping, the texture (subtle grain + hairlines), and the choreographed rhythm — not from color saturation or effects.
- **Never retailer/affiliate/medical** because there is *no* product photography hero, *no* price/badge/star vocabulary, *no* loud conversion color, and the medical-caution material is rendered as a calm explanatory diagram (amber, soft) rather than a warning-red alert or anatomy chart. The field-guide metaphor is inherently educational and brand-neutral — a field guide describes the territory; it doesn't sell you a map.

The tension we're managing: **alive vs. calm.** "The Quiet Field Guide" resolves it — richness lives in *craft and texture* (illustration, paper, line, rhythm), while *calm* lives in color discipline and spacing. We get visual interest without visual noise.

## PART B — THE ELEVATED VISUAL SYSTEM

### 1. Visual Identity & Art-Direction Principles

Seven non-negotiable principles. Every design decision must satisfy these.

1. **Paper, ink, and one careful hand.** The site behaves like a printed reference: warm paper surfaces, charcoal ink, hairline rules, and a single consistent "drawn" hand across all illustration and iconography. Nothing looks rendered, glossy, or 3D.
2. **Diagrams over decoration.** Every illustration *explains something* — a checklist, a comparison, a showroom layout. We never draw mood-art. If a visual doesn't carry information, it's cut. This is what keeps us editorial, not promotional.
3. **Hierarchy is built with scale and weight, not color.** Color is for wayfinding and accent only. The eye is led by type size, weight, and whitespace.
4. **Restraint is the brand.** Two accents plus one caution amber. Ever. Adding a fourth accent is a system violation. The discipline *is* the premium signal.
5. **Rhythm is composed, not repeated.** Sections alternate loud/quiet, dense/open, surface/full-bleed. No two adjacent sections share the same shape.
6. **Independence is visible.** No product hero, no logos, no prices, no stars, no "best of," no urgency. The visual vocabulary itself enforces neutrality.
7. **Calm authority on every claim.** Medical-adjacent content is rendered soft, explanatory, amber-toned — never alarmist red, never authoritative blue-medical, never anatomical.

### 2. Color Palette — roles, hex, contrast

I'm evolving the existing palette, not discarding it. The pine and clay were *right in spirit but wrong in execution* (too few steps, too saturated for the clay, surfaces too close). Changes and justification below.

#### Surfaces (the paper) — *now with real value steps*

| Token | Hex | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--surface-paper` | `#FBFAF6` | Primary page surface (warmer than pure white) | Replaces #FFFFFF — pure white felt cold/screeny and broke the paper metaphor |
| `--surface-raised` | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, raised elements (now the *bright* surface, used sparingly) | White becomes a tool for elevation, not the base |
| `--surface-sand` | `#F2EFE6` | Alternating section band (clearly distinct now) | Deepened from #F6F4EF so the alternation is *visible* |
| `--surface-clay-tint` | `#ECE6DB` | Quiet emphasis band, deep editorial sections | New, deeper step — gives the page a real low end |
| `--surface-ink-deep` | `#23272A` | Full-bleed inverted bands (footer, signature quotes) | New — a dark surface for dramatic rhythm contrast |

**Why this matters:** the old three surfaces spanned a value range of roughly 96→94→93 (nearly invisible). The new set spans roughly 98→95→92→89, *plus* a near-black band. The alternation now reads at a glance, and the dark band gives us a "loud" moment for rhythm.

#### Inks (the type)

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast on `--surface-paper` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--ink-primary` | `#1C1F1D` | Display headlines, primary body | ~15.8:1 — AAA |
| `--ink-body` | `#33383B` | Long-form body text | ~10.5:1 — AAA |
| `--ink-muted` | `#5C615F` | Captions, metadata, secondary | ~5.6:1 — AA (use ≥16px) |
| `--ink-faint` | `#8A8E8A` | Hairline labels, disabled, dividers-as-text | ~3.2:1 — UI/large text only, never body |
| `--ink-on-dark` | `#F4F2EC` | Text on `--surface-ink-deep` | ~13:1 on #23272A — AAA |

#### Accents — *evolved pine + refined terracotta*

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--accent-pine` | `#1E5A4C` | **Primary.** CTAs, active states, key links, primary illustration line | White text on pine ~6.4:1 — AA (incl. large/AAA-adjacent). Kept the pine; nudged very slightly to read richer/less gray |
| `--accent-pine-deep` | `#143F35` | Pine hover/pressed, pine on dark surfaces | Darker step for state changes |
| `--accent-pine-tint` | `#DCE7E1` | Pine illustration fill, soft section wash, selected-card tint | Tint-fill for the illustration system |
| `--accent-terracotta` | `#B65F40` | **Secondary.** Eyebrows/labels, illustration secondary line, editorial accent | On paper ~4.6:1 — AA for large/label text. **Slightly desaturated from #A8553B** so it reads "fired clay," not "rust-orange" (the old clay flirted with the forbidden sales-orange) |
| `--accent-terracotta-tint` | `#F0E2D8` | Terracotta tint-fill in illustrations, eyebrow background chips | Soft fill only |

**Critical color rule:** Terracotta is a *label and line* color, **never** a CTA or button fill. CTAs are pine, always. This prevents any drift toward the warm sales-button look. Terracotta lives at eyebrow scale and inside illustrations.

#### Caution amber (medical-claim system) — *calm, never alarmist*

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--caution-amber` | `#C68A2E` | Medical-caution accent line, "claim-awareness" markers | On paper ~3.4:1 — large/icon use; pair with ink text, never color-alone |
| `--caution-amber-tint` | `#F6ECD6` | Caution section background wash, callout fill | Warm, soft — reads "note," not "alarm" |
| `--caution-ink` | `#6B4E16` | Text inside amber callouts | On amber-tint ~6.8:1 — AA |

**Why amber, not red/orange:** The brief forbids alarmist medical authority *and* sales urgency. A muted, warm amber on a soft sand-tint reads as "a thoughtful margin note" — the universal language of "read this carefully," not "danger." It harmonizes with terracotta (same warm family) so the caution section feels *part of the system*, not bolted on. Never use red, never use a warning triangle filled solid, never use a medical cross.

**Color-never list:** pure saturated red, orange (#FF6B00 family), any neon, medical blue (#0066CC family), gradient buttons, color used as the *only* signal for state.

### 3. Typography

Pairing: an **editorial display serif** with a **humanist sans**, both with robust fallbacks. I'm specifying the *roles* and a recommended typeface set; substitute within the same classification if licensing requires.

#### Typefaces

- **Display / Serif:** **Fraunces** (variable, optical sizing) — a warm, slightly "old-style" display serif with high-contrast strokes and a literary, field-guide warmth. Alternates: Source Serif 4, Newsreader.
  Stack: `"Fraunces", "Source Serif 4", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif`
- **Text / Sans:** **Inter** or **IBM Plex Sans** — clean humanist sans for body, UI, labels. Plex leans more "documented/technical-trust"; Inter leans more neutral-modern. **Recommend IBM Plex Sans** for the analyst-desk character.
  Stack: `"IBM Plex Sans", "Inter", -apple-system, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif`
- **Mono (sparingly):** **IBM Plex Mono** — for data labels, "spec" tags, checklist numbering, comparison-table column heads. Adds the *reference-document* texture. Use at small sizes only.
  Stack: `"IBM Plex Mono", "SF Mono", ui-monospace, monospace`

#### Type ramp (mobile-first; desktop in parens where it scales)

| Token | Size mobile → desktop | Line-height | Weight | Family | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--t-display` | 40 → 68px | 1.04 | 340 (light) | Fraunces | Hero headline — *light weight is the move* |
| `--t-h1` | 32 → 48px | 1.1 | 400 | Fraunces | Page/section opener |
| `--t-h2` | 26 → 36px | 1.15 | 460 | Fraunces | Section headlines |
| `--t-h3` | 21 → 26px | 1.25 | 520 | Fraunces *or* Plex Sans 600 | Card titles, sub-sections |
| `--t-lead` | 18 → 21px | 1.5 | 400 | Plex Sans | Intro/answer-block lead paragraph |
| `--t-body` | 16 → 17px | 1.65 | 400 | Plex Sans | Body text |
| `--t-small` | 14 → 15px | 1.55 | 400 | Plex Sans | Captions, FAQ meta |
| `--t-eyebrow` | 12 → 13px | 1.2 | 600 | Plex Mono | Eyebrows/labels |
| `--t-tag` | 11 → 12px | 1.2 | 500 | Plex Mono | Data tags, table heads |

**The hierarchy fix in one line:** display (68) ÷ body (17) = **4×** contrast, plus the display is *light* (340) against *bold* (600) eyebrows. That single decision — big light serif over small bold mono eyebrow — instantly reads "premium editorial" and kills the flatness.

#### Eyebrow / label treatment (signature element)

The eyebrow is a brand signature, not an afterthought:
- Plex **Mono**, 12–13px, `letter-spacing: 0.12em`, uppercase, `--accent-terracotta`.
- Preceded by a **2px × 18px terracotta tick mark** (a short horizontal rule), not a dot. This little tick is a recurring motif — it appears before every eyebrow, echoing a field-guide entry marker.
- Optionally numbered for sequential sections (`01 — START HERE`, `02 — BUYING CHECKLIST`) in mono, reinforcing the "guide with chapters" feel.

#### Measure & rhythm
- Body measure: **62–70 characters** max (`max-width: 36rem` for prose blocks).
- Lead paragraphs: up to 75ch, slightly looser.
- Never set body full-bleed across a wide container — always constrain to the reading column even when the section is full-width.

### 4. Spacing, Grid & Layout

- **Base unit:** `4px`. All spacing is a multiple. Spacing scale (tokens): `4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128, 160`.
- **Vertical rhythm:** section vertical padding steps with importance — quiet sections `64px` (mobile) / `96px` (desktop); standard `96 / 128`; hero and signature bands `128 / 160`. *Varying section padding is itself a rhythm tool* — don't pad every section equally.
- **Container:** content max-width `1200px`; reading column `680px`; wide editorial (comparison tables, full illustrations) `1080px`. Gutters: `20px` mobile, `40px` tablet, `64px` desktop.
- **Grid:** 12-column on desktop (≥1024), 8-column tablet, 4-column mobile, `24px` gutter. Most editorial content lives in a 7–8 column reading zone offset within the 12, leaving an asymmetric margin — the "marginalia" lane where tick-marks, numbers, and small annotations live. *Asymmetry is intentional and on-brand* (field-guide margins).
- **Breakpoints:** `480 / 768 / 1024 / 1280`.
- **The marginalia lane:** on desktop, reserve a ~120px left or right column for eyebrow numbers, small annotation lines, and the terracotta tick. This is what makes the layout feel like a *documented reference* rather than a centered blog.

### 5. Card Style — and the "Not-a-Product-Card" Rule

**Anatomy (top to bottom):** illustration or icon zone → mono tag/number → H3 title (serif or sans-600) → 1–2 line body → text-link with arrow (never a filled button inside a card).

**Tokens:**
- Radius: `--radius-card: 14px` (soft but not pill; product cards are usually 8px sharp or 24px pill — we sit deliberately between).
- Border: `1px solid #E6E2D8` (a warm hairline, *not* gray). On `--surface-raised` white cards over a sand band.
- Elevation: **flat by default.** `box-shadow: none` at rest — a hairline border does the separation. This is critical: shadows read as "app UI / ecommerce tile."
- Hover (pointer devices only): border deepens to `--accent-pine-tint` edge, a `2px` terracotta tick animates in at top-left, and the card lifts `2px` with a *very* soft shadow `0 6px 20px rgba(28,31,29,0.06)`. Subtle. The arrow on the link nudges `4px` right.
- Internal padding: `24px` mobile, `32px` desktop.

**The Not-a-Product-Card Rule (mandatory):**
A card on this site must NEVER contain: a price, a price placeholder, a star rating, a "buy"/"shop"/"view deal" button, a product photograph as the dominant element, a brand logo, a discount/percentage badge, a stock/inventory cue, or a colored corner ribbon. Cards carry *guidance and pathways*, illustrated by our line-system, leading to *articles*. If a card starts to resemble a retail tile in a critique, redesign it. The tell of a product card is **photo-top + price + button**; we are **illustration-top + topic + text-link**.

### 6. Icon Style

Custom set — retire all default stroke libraries.

- **Construction:** **two-weight line.** Primary strokes `2px`, secondary/interior strokes `1.25px`. This dual weight is what makes them look *drawn*, not generated.
- Stroke color: `--ink-primary` for most; key icons may use `--accent-pine`. Caution-system icons use `--caution-amber` line with `--caution-ink` detail.
- Caps & joins: round cap, round join. Corner radius on rectangular forms `~2px`.
- Grid: 24px box, 1.5px keyline padding. Optical balance over mathematical.
- **Signature detail:** a single small **terracotta tint-fill accent** inside each icon (one shape filled `--accent-terracotta-tint`) — e.g., the checkmark inside a clipboard, the seat cushion in a chair glyph. One spot of warm fill per icon ties icons to the illustration family.
- Forbidden glyphs: medical cross, stethoscope, heartbeat line, shopping cart, price tag, star, trophy/award, megaphone, flame/"hot."

### 7. Illustration Language — the single cohesive family

**This is the most important section. All 10 section visuals must obey it so they read as one hand.**

**Name:** *"Pen-and-Paper Diagrams."*

**The rules (memorize these — consistency here is the entire game):**

1. **Two-weight line.** Outlines `2px`, interior/detail lines `1.25px`. Same as icons, scaled up. Round caps and joins throughout.
2. **Soft tint-fill, never flat color, never gradient.** Fills come only from the tint tokens: `--accent-pine-tint`, `--accent-terracotta-tint`, `--caution-amber-tint`, plus a neutral `#ECE8DE`. Fills are *under* the lines and slightly *offset* (1–2px) from the outline — the classic "color printed slightly off-register" field-guide look. This offset is a signature; it makes everything feel hand-printed.
3. **Limited palette per illustration:** line ink + at most **two** tint-fills + one accent line color. Never more.
4. **Flat, axonometric, or gentle 2.5° "isometric-lite" perspective** — no realistic vanishing-point perspective, no drop shadows, no 3D rendering. Objects sit on subtle ground-lines or float with a thin contact ellipse.
5. **Diagrammatic intent always.** Each illustration shows a *relationship or process*: a checklist with animated ticks, a showroom floorplan from above, an "online vs showroom" split panel, a warranty document with highlighted clauses, a comparison matrix abstracted into shapes. The chair itself appears as a *simplified line glyph*, never a photoreal render and never a specific identifiable model.
6. **Optional paper grain:** a `2–4%` opacity noise/grain texture overlay on illustration backgrounds only, never on text. Lazy-loaded; omitted under reduced-data.
7. **Annotation marks:** thin terracotta leader-lines, small mono labels, and dotted measure-lines (like a field-guide pointing out features). These tie illustration to typography.

**The 10 section visuals, all in this language:**
1. *Hero* — the field-guide "decision map" (see hero spec below).
2. *Start Here* — three diverging pen-paths from a single point to three labeled destinations.
3. *Buying checklist* — a clipboard sheet with line-drawn rows and animated ticks.
4. *Try-before-buy* — a simplified chair glyph with a seated figure outline and "comfort zones" annotated with leader-lines.
5. *Retailer/showroom trust* — a storefront-from-above floorplan with trust "checkpoints" marked.
6. *Review interpretation* — abstracted review cards with a magnifier and a small distribution curve (not stars).
7. *Medical-claim caution* — amber margin-note treatment: a document with certain claim-phrases highlighted amber and a calm "?" annotation. No body/anatomy.
8. *Online vs showroom* — a split diptych panel, screen-glyph vs floorplan-glyph, balanced (neutral, not favoring one).
9. *Local showroom guide* — a simplified area map with location pins as our custom glyph and a "things to ask" tab.
10. *Brand/model comparison* — an abstract matrix of shape-rows and attribute-columns, deliberately **unbranded** (rows are "Model A/B/C" silhouettes, never logos, never ranked best-to-worst).

**Consistency enforcement:** one illustrator (or one set of master components) draws all ten. Build a shared component kit: the chair glyph, the figure outline, the document/sheet, the pin, the path-line, the tick, the leader-line, the grain. Everything assembles from these primitives.

### 8. Photography Direction

Photography is **secondary and atmospheric** — the illustrations carry information; photos carry *warmth and reality*. Use sparingly (editorial trust section, maybe hero texture, author/about).

**Shoot / source:**
- Calm, warm-lit interior environments: a quiet showroom corner, soft daylight, neutral tones that match the palette.
- Hands and texture: a hand resting on a chair armrest, fabric/leather close-ups, a notebook with handwritten questions — *process and consideration*, not product glamour.
- Honest, candid human moments: a person *thinking*/comparing, not delighted-customer stock.
- Muted, slightly warm grade; matte, low-contrast finish; film-adjacent. No HDR, no glossy highlights.

**Never shoot / never source:**
- Hero product shots of a single chair on white (ecommerce signal #1).
- Handshakes, sales-office, salesperson-with-clipboard, "happy purchase" stock.
- Before/after, anyone in pain or relief poses, anything medical/clinical, massage-of-a-person imagery.
- Identifiable brand chairs/logos/showroom signage.
- Stars, prices, screens showing prices, "as seen on."
- Stock-photo grin / thumbs-up / fake-diverse-team-pointing-at-laptop.

**Treatment:** all photos get a subtle warm duotone option toward `--ink-primary`/`--surface-sand` for cohesion, and may sit behind a thin hairline frame with a small mono caption — making even photos feel like *plates in a reference book*.

### 9. Motion Style

**Philosophy:** motion *confirms and explains*; it never performs. If an animation doesn't help comprehension or provide gentle life, it's cut. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (disable transforms/loops, keep instant state changes).

**Signature motions (the recurring vocabulary):**
1. **Line-draw on scroll** — illustrations draw their `2px` outlines via `stroke-dashoffset` as they enter the viewport (once, ~700ms, ease-out). Tint-fills fade in 150ms after the line completes. This is *the* signature — every illustration "draws itself" like a hand sketching.
2. **Animated tick** — checklist ticks draw in sequence (60ms stagger), each ~250ms. Used in the checklist, in cards on hover, anywhere we confirm.
3. **The terracotta tick-mark wipe** — eyebrows' tick-rule wipes in left-to-right (200ms) on section reveal.
4. **Soft float** — hero cards and pins drift `±3px` on a slow 6–8s sine loop, *very* subtle, paused under reduced-motion.
5. **Arrow nudge** — link arrows translate `4px` on hover (120ms).
6. **Accordion** — FAQ expands with height + opacity ease (250ms), chevron rotates 180°.
7. **Progress indicator** — a thin pine progress rule under the section chapter-number as the user scrolls a long section.

**Forbidden:** parallax depth scrolling, scale-bounce, autoplay video with sound, anything over ~700ms, scroll-jacking, particle effects, looping motion that never rests, motion that blocks content from rendering.

**Silent micro-video concept (optional, lazy, fallback-to-still):** a 4–6s silent loop of the hero "decision map" drawing itself and one card gently floating — but **default to the SVG animation**; video only as progressive enhancement, poster-image fallback always present.

### 10. Background, Texture & Hairlines

- **Alternating surfaces** drive rhythm: `paper → sand → paper → clay-tint → ink-deep (signature band) → paper`. Never two identical adjacent surfaces.
- **Grain:** an optional `2–3%` opacity paper-grain on `sand` and `clay-tint` bands (CSS `background-image` noise or a tiny tiled PNG). Omitted on `paper` (keep reading crisp) and under reduced-data/reduced-motion.
- **Hairlines:** `1px` warm rules (`#E2DDD2`) separate major zones — but used *sparingly*, as a field-guide uses rules between entries. A full-bleed hairline at section tops, sometimes with a small mono chapter-number sitting on it (like a running header).
- **Editorial margins:** the asymmetric marginalia lane (see grid) carries chapter numbers, tick-marks, and the occasional vertical mono label rotated 90° — a premium-magazine device, used once or twice per page, not everywhere.
- **The dark signature band** (`--surface-ink-deep`) appears exactly **once or twice** — ideal for the "Editorial trust / how we work / independence" statement and/or the final soft CTA. It's the page's dramatic low note; overusing it kills the effect.

### 11. Section Rhythm — the choreography

The fix for monotony is a *composed* sequence. Map of intended dynamics down the page:

| Section | Surface | Density | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header/nav | paper | minimal | sticky, hairline-bottom on scroll |
| **Hero** | paper + grain | **medium-loud** | big light serif + decision-map illustration; *focal*, asymmetric |
| Intro answer block | paper | **quiet** | single reading column, lead paragraph — a deliberate breath after the hero |
| Start Here pathways | sand | medium | 3 path-cards, illustration of diverging paths |
| Buying checklist | paper | **dense** | clipboard illustration + animated checklist — a "working" section |
| Try-before-buy | clay-tint | medium | chair+figure illustration, comfort-zone annotations |
| Retailer/showroom trust | paper | medium | floorplan illustration |
| Review interpretation | sand | medium-dense | abstracted reviews + distribution curve |
| **Medical-claim caution** | caution-amber-tint | **quiet, calm** | amber margin-note treatment; deliberately soft and spacious |
| Online vs showroom | paper | medium | balanced diptych |
| Local showroom guide | sand | medium | map illustration |
| Brand/model comparison | paper | **dense** | abstract matrix, unbranded |
| **Editorial trust / independence** | **ink-deep** | **loud** | the dark signature band — our credo, big serif on dark |
| FAQ | paper | medium | accordion, mono numbering |
| Related guides | sand | medium | index-style link list, field-guide "see also" |
| **Final soft CTA** | clay-tint *or* ink-deep | medium-loud | calm invitation: "Start with the checklist" — pine button, no urgency |

The principle: **after every dense or loud section comes a quiet one.** The amber caution section is intentionally the *calmest* on the page — restraint there reinforces "we're being careful, not scaring you." The two dark bands are the only true crescendos.

### 12. Mobile-First Visual Behavior

- **Type:** ramp top values are mobile values (display 40px, body 16px). The light-serif/bold-mono-eyebrow contrast holds at small sizes — it's the cheapest hierarchy win on a phone.
- **Illustrations:** every one has a *mobile crop / simplified variant* — the floorplan and matrix especially must reflow to a single-column, vertically-readable form, not a shrunk-down wide diagram. Author each illustration in two compositions: wide (≥768) and tall (mobile).
- **Marginalia lane collapses:** chapter numbers and ticks move *inline above* the eyebrow on mobile (the asymmetric margin is a desktop luxury).
- **Cards stack** full-width, `16px` gutters, hover effects become *tap/active* states (the tick draws on tap).
- **Motion:** line-draw and tick animations stay (they're cheap, GPU-light SVG); floats and the optional video are **off** on mobile by default to protect battery/performance and because small floats read as jitter. Reduced-motion always wins.
- **Surfaces:** keep the alternation — value steps are even more important on small screens where there's less whitespace to create separation.
- **Performance:** illustrations are inline SVG (themeable, animatable, light); grain textures and any photography lazy-load below the fold; the dark band and full illustrations defer until near-viewport. Target: no layout shift, illustrations never block first paint.
- **Touch targets:** all interactive elements ≥44px; FAQ rows, path-cards, and links generously tappable.

## 7. Motion idea

Choreographed, ~under 1.4s total, runs **once** on load/scroll-in, then rests:

1. **Plates draw in (the signature):** the desk plane and the five plate outlines draw via `stroke-dashoffset`, staggered ~80ms, dominant checklist sheet first (so the eye is led to the focal object), ~600ms total, ease-out.
2. **Tint-fills fade up** 150ms after each plate's outline completes (the off-register fill "prints").
3. **Ticks animate:** the two pre-checked checklist ticks draw in sequence (60ms stagger, ~250ms each) in pine — the system's "confirm" motion, previewing the checklist section.
4. **Leader-lines wipe** left-to-right (200ms) and the mono annotation labels fade in.
5. **Eyebrow tick-rule** wipes in beside the eyebrow (200ms), synced with headline fade-up.
6. **At rest:** the dominant checklist plate and one tab perform the *very* subtle `±3px` 7s sine float; everything else is still. No looping line motion, no particles.

**Hover (pointer only):** hovering the primary button nudges nothing in the art (calm); hovering the desk gently lifts the checklist plate `2px` with the soft `0 6px 20px rgba(28,31,29,0.06)` shadow and the `CHECKLIST` tab tick draws — a quiet invitation toward the CTA.

**`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:** no draw, no float, no wipes. The fully-rendered SVG (all lines, fills, ticks present) appears immediately; only opacity may settle instantly. Identical information, zero motion.

## 6. Recommended format
**Interactive inline-SVG checklist (progressive enhancement over a static SVG base).** Justification:

- **Inline SVG** keeps it lightweight, themeable via the design tokens, crisp on all DPRs, and animatable for the line-draw and tick signatures — exactly the system's preferred format.
- **Interactive** is the right call *specifically here* because the felt sense of *progress* is the soft-conversion mechanic. Letting the user actually tick items (state stored in `localStorage`, never sent anywhere) makes the tool *theirs* and delivers the "equipped and in motion" feeling no static image can. - **Graceful base:** server-renders as a fully readable static checklist (a few items pre-ticked to demonstrate the mechanic). If JS is off, it's still a complete, valuable, accessible list — interactivity only enhances. No functionality is gated behind script.

Not a video (state must be real and persistent), not a static image (loses the progress mechanic), not a lead form (forbidden).

## 7. Motion idea
Restrained, comprehension-first, all within ~700ms ceilings:

- **On scroll into view (once):** the sheet's `2px` outline draws itself via `stroke-dashoffset` (~700ms ease-out), then row hairlines fade in left-to-right (60ms stagger), then 2–3 demo items tick in sequence — each tick draws in ~250ms with a 60ms stagger, the pine-tint fill fading 150ms behind the stroke. The progress meter counts up in step (`0→3 / 12`) and its pine rule fills to match. The terracotta eyebrow tick wipes in left-to-right (200ms).
- **On user tick (interaction):** the checkmark draws (~250ms), pine-tint fill fades in, the row label gets a subtle `--ink-muted` strike-through-free de-emphasis (slight opacity to 0.7, never a literal strikethrough — keep it readable), and the progress meter increments with a 200ms rule extension. A single, silent, sub-3px settle — no bounce, no confetti, no sound.
- **Hover (pointer only):** checkbox border deepens to `--accent-pine-tint` edge; the footer link arrow nudges 4px right (120ms).
- **prefers-reduced-motion: reduce:** no stroke-draw, no count-up animation, no float; items appear in final state, ticks toggle instantly, progress updates instantly. All meaning is preserved — motion is purely confirmatory.

## 7. Motion idea

Restrained, comprehension-first, all `≤700ms`:

- **On scroll-into-view (once):** the big `4.6` card draws its outline, then the **three pen-paths line-draw outward** left-to-right (~600ms, 80ms stagger between paths), and the three lens cards fade up `+4px` in sequence (120ms stagger). The terracotta divergence tick wipes in. This literally *performs the split* — the core idea animates itself.
- **Distribution micro-bars:** each bar's segments grow from baseline (~250ms) just after its column resolves, so the reader sees the *different shapes* appear.
- **Optional interaction (pointer + keyboard):** hovering/focusing a lens column gently raises it (`2px`, soft `0 6px 20px rgba(28,31,29,0.06)`), draws a `2px` terracotta tick at its corner, and *dims the other two to ~55% opacity* so one lens reads at a time — reinforcing "isolate the signal." Tab cycles the three lenses; Enter/Space pins one.
- **Arrow/leader-line** on the problem-statement nudges `4px` on hover.

**prefers-reduced-motion: reduce →** no path-draw, no fades, no bar-growth, no dimming; everything renders in final state instantly, paths and bars shown complete. Interaction still pins/highlights but via instant opacity change, no transform.

## Asset 7 — Medical-Claim Caution Visual


## 1. Visual name
**"The Margin Note"** — a calm, field-guide-style annotation that teaches readers to *read the words on the box*, not a warning label that scares them away from it.

## 2. Purpose
To give buyers a simple, reusable literacy tool: how to tell the difference between **comfort/wellness language** (safe, normal, expected on a massage chair) and **treatment-style language** (a signal to slow down and ask for evidence). It teaches a *reading skill*, not a verdict. The visual must make claim-awareness feel like a calm editorial habit — the way a careful reference book teaches you to read a footnote — and must never imply that any chair is dangerous, fraudulent, or medically endorsed. Its quiet "?" evidence-prompt converts "this chair improves your health" into "what is that statement based on?" — the single most useful question a buyer can carry into a showroom.

## 3. Where it appears on the homepage
The **Medical-claim caution** section — positioned per the canonical rhythm map immediately after *Review interpretation* (dense, sand) and before *Online vs showroom* (medium, paper). This is the page's deliberate **calm trough**: surface `--caution-amber-tint` (#F6ECD6), the most spacious and slowest section on the page. Its role in the choreography is decompression — after the reader has worked through reviews, this section *opens up*, sets a single reading column wide, and speaks gently. The visual reinforces that calm: it is the one illustration on the page that should feel almost still, like a hand pausing over a page to underline one phrase. It distinguishes itself from the nine siblings by being the only **text-as-subject** illustration — where others diagram chairs, floorplans, and paths, this one diagrams *language itself*.

## 4. What it should look like
A single, generous **field-guide page spread** rendered in the Pen-and-Paper Diagrams language — as if we photographed one open leaf of the guide and annotated it by hand.

**Composition (desktop, ~16:9 within the 1080px editorial width, offset right inside the 12-col grid so the marginalia lane sits left):**

- **Background:** the amber-tint section surface, with a faint 2–3% paper grain. A single hairline `--rule` (#E2DDD2) frames a "specimen sheet" panel on `--surface-raised` white, sitting slightly off-register (1–2px) from its own outline — the signature hand-printed offset.

- **Layer 1 — the specimen sheet (center-left mass):** a line-drawn document with three or four "claim cards" stacked as rows, each a simplified quotation block drawn in `1.25px` interior line. Each row holds a short phrase rendered in real type (Fraunces italic, `--ink-body`) as if clipped from a brochure. To the left of each phrase sits a small **two-state marker** — never a checkmark/cross pair (that reads as right/wrong/medical pass-fail). Instead:
  - **Comfort/wellness phrases** carry a small **pine tick-leaf** (a single soft `--accent-pine` stroke, the same tick motif from eyebrows) and a faint `--accent-pine-tint` underline-wash under the phrase — meaning *"normal language, expected."*
  - **Treatment-style phrases** carry a small **amber "?" annotation glyph** in `--caution-amber` with a thin terracotta leader-line pointing out into the right margin — meaning *"pause and ask."* The phrase itself gets a soft `--caution-amber-tint` highlighter-wash (like a hand-drawn highlighter run, slightly overshooting the text edges — the off-register signature again).

- **Layer 2 — the margin annotation (right margin / marginalia lane):** the terracotta leader-lines from the treatment phrases converge on a single hand-written-feeling note: a small mono label `WHAT IS THIS BASED ON?` set in IBM Plex Mono with a hand-drawn amber underline. Below it, a soft amber **"?" in a hand-drawn circle** (open stroke, never a filled warning triangle, never a solid alert dot) acts as the visual anchor — the calm "evidence question" made into a mark you can carry away.

- **Layer 3 — the two-column legend (bottom strip):** a quiet, balanced diptych — left header `LANGUAGE THAT'S NORMAL` (pine tick), right header `LANGUAGE TO QUESTION` (amber ?). Deliberately **equal weight and equal size** — neither column is "bad," one is just a cue to ask. No red, no skull, no alarm; the amber column is exactly as calm as the pine one.

**Palette usage & hierarchy:** the page is overwhelmingly paper + ink; pine and amber appear *only* as the two marker states and their washes; terracotta appears *only* as leader-lines and the mono margin label (its system-correct label-and-line role). The visual reads charcoal-and-paper first, with two small warm temperature cues second — exactly the restraint that keeps it editorial. Hierarchy: the highlighted treatment-phrase + its "?" annotation is the single focal point; everything else is supporting diagram.

**Inline animated SVG illustration** (not video, not static image, not a true interactive). # Parts 5–8 — Visual System, Mobile, Accessibility/Performance & Priorities

## 5. Visual system recommendations

**Icon style — retire all default stroke libraries.** Custom **two-weight line** set: primary strokes 2px, secondary/interior 1.25px — the dual weight is what makes them read *drawn*, not generated. Round caps and joins; ~2px corner radius on rectangular forms; 24px box with 1.5px keyline padding; optical balance over mathematical. Stroke color `--ink-primary` (key icons may use `--accent-pine`). **Signature detail:** exactly one small `--accent-terracotta-tint` fill shape per icon (the checkmark inside a clipboard, the cushion in a chair glyph) — one warm spot ties icons to the illustration family. **Forbidden glyphs:** medical cross, stethoscope, heartbeat line, shopping cart, price tag, star, trophy/award, megaphone, flame/"hot."

**Illustration style — the single cohesive family, "Pen-and-Paper Diagrams."** This is the whole game; all ten assets obey it so they read as one hand. Rules: (1) two-weight line, 2px outlines / 1.25px detail, round caps and joins; (2) **soft tint-fills, never flat color, never gradient** — fills come only from the tint tokens, sit *under* the lines, and are *offset 1–2px* (the off-register hand-printed signature); (3) at most two tint-fills + one accent line per illustration; (4) flat / axonometric / gentle 2.5° "isometric-lite," no realistic perspective, no drop shadows, no 3D; (5) **diagrammatic intent always** — every illustration shows a relationship or process, and the chair appears only as a simplified line glyph, never photoreal, never an identifiable model; (6) optional 2–4% paper grain on illustration backgrounds only (never on text), lazy-loaded; (7) thin terracotta leader-lines, small mono labels, dotted measure-lines as annotation. Enforcement: one illustrator or one master component kit draws all ten, assembling from the shared primitives.

**Motion style — confirms and explains, never performs.** Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Vocabulary: **line-draw on scroll** (`stroke-dashoffset`, ~700ms ease-out, once, fills fade 150ms behind); **animated tick** (sequential, 60ms stagger, ~250ms each); **terracotta tick-rule wipe** on eyebrows (200ms); **arrow nudge** on hover (4px, 120ms); **accordion** (height + opacity, 250ms); subtle **±2–3px float** on a few hero/map elements (slow sine, desktop only). **The signature is rationed** (the key correction): only three landmark assets fully "draw themselves" — the Hero (A1), the Buying Checklist (A3), and the Field Index (A10a). Every other asset enters with a quieter fade, a short fill, or animates only on interaction, and most below-fold assets default to static under any data/perf constraint. Global caps: nothing over ~700ms, no loops that never rest. **Forbidden:** parallax depth scroll, scale-bounce, autoplay-with-sound, scroll-jacking, particles, motion that blocks render or implies a ranking emerging.

**Photography direction — secondary and atmospheric.** Illustrations carry information; photos carry warmth and reality, used sparingly (editorial trust, author/about, occasional texture). **Source:** calm warm-lit interiors, soft daylight, palette-matched neutrals; hands and texture (a hand on an armrest, fabric close-ups, a notebook of handwritten questions); honest candid people *thinking/comparing*. Treatment: muted warm grade, matte low-contrast, film-adjacent; optional warm duotone toward `--ink-primary`/`--surface-sand`; a thin hairline frame with a small mono caption so even photos read as plates in a reference book. **Never:** hero product-on-white, handshakes/sales-office/"happy purchase" stock, before/after or pain/relief poses, anything medical/clinical, identifiable brand chairs/logos/signage, stars/prices/"as seen on," thumbs-up grin stock.

**Color direction (with hex).** Surfaces step in real value: `--surface-paper #FBFAF6`, `--surface-raised #FDFCF8`, `--surface-bright #FFFFFF` (interactive hover only), `--surface-sand #F2EFE6`, `--surface-clay-tint #ECE6DB`, `--surface-ink-deep #1A1D1B`. Inks: `--ink-primary #1C1F1D`, `--ink-body #33383B`, `--ink-muted #5C615F` (≥16px), `--ink-faint #8A8E8A` (lines only), `--ink-on-dark #F4F2EC`. Accents: `--accent-pine #1E5A4C` (CTA/links/primary line), `--accent-pine-deep #143F35` (states), `--accent-pine-tint #DCE7E1` (fill); `--accent-terracotta #B65F40` (ticks/lines/markers), `--accent-terracotta-deep #8F4226` (the only terracotta as small text), `--accent-terracotta-tint #F0E2D8` (fill). Caution: `--caution-amber #C68A2E` (markers only), `--caution-amber-tint #F6ECD6` (band), `--caution-ink #5E430E` (all caution text). Hairlines `--rule #E2DDD2`, card border `#E6E2D8`. Two accents + one caution amber, ever; terracotta and amber are line/marker colors, never the sole color of small text.

**Typography direction.** Fraunces (variable, optical-size) for display/headings; IBM Plex Sans for body/UI; IBM Plex Mono for eyebrows, tags, chapter numbers. The ramp runs display 40→68px down to body 16→17px — a ~4× display-to-body contrast, paired *light serif over bold mono eyebrow*. ## 6. Mobile behavior recommendations

- **Type:** ramp top values are the mobile values; the light-serif-over-bold-mono-eyebrow contrast is the cheapest hierarchy win on a phone, and the display steps up to 420 weight below 480px to stay legible.
- **Illustrations are authored in two compositions, not shrunk.** Every asset has a *tall* variant: the Trailhead threads cards on a vertical stub; the Fit Map becomes illustration-plus-numbered-list with ring numbers linking the two (no color/leader dependency); the floorplan/region maps reorient top-to-bottom; the Field Index becomes stacked entity-cards with a "Compare by attribute" toggle; the Honest Spread becomes a labeled lens carousel; the Two Doors stacks with the bridge node on a horizontal spine; the Margin Note relocates its margin annotation into a full-width callout so nothing points off-screen.
- **Marginalia collapses inline:** chapter numbers and ticks move above the eyebrow (the asymmetric margin is a desktop luxury).
- **Cards stack** full-width, 16px gutters; hover effects become tap/active states; all interactive targets ≥44px (FAQ rows, path-cards, links, checkboxes, toggles).
- **Motion trims:** line-draw and tick animations stay (cheap, GPU-light SVG); floats and any optional video are off on mobile by default; reduced-motion always wins.
- **Surfaces keep alternating** — value stepping matters *more* on small screens where there's less whitespace to separate sections.
- **Performance:** illustrations are inline SVG (themeable, light); grain and photography lazy-load below the fold; the dark band and full illustrations defer until near-viewport; reserved aspect-ratio boxes prevent layout shift so illustrations never block first paint.

## 7. Accessibility and performance recommendations

**Accessibility.** Verified contrast governs everything: every accent-as-text pairing was re-measured at its actual size against the final surface hex, and terracotta/amber are forbidden as the sole color of small text (use `--accent-terracotta-deep #8F4226` or `--caution-ink #5E430E`). **Never color-alone** — every state carries a redundant shape/text/position cue (the active Trailhead route is color *and* tick *and* lift *and* arrow; review lenses are icon *and* tag *and* position; comparison cells are monochrome shapes; the caution states are pine-tick-leaf-plus-"NORMAL" vs amber-"?"-plus-"QUESTION"). All illustration text that carries meaning is **real, selectable HTML/SVG text**, not baked pixels — translatable, zoomable, SR-readable, and answer-engine-indexable. The comparison is a real `<table>` with proper scope; the checklist uses native `<input type="checkbox">` + `<label>` and a `role="progressbar"`; routes are a real `<nav>`/`<ul>` of `<a>` links with the decorative SVG `aria-hidden`. Keyboard: every interactive element is focusable in logical order with a visible 2px pine focus ring + offset; no focus traps; toggles are real `button`s with `aria-pressed`. The medical caution carries no alarm semantics (no `role="alert"`, no warning triangle, no red), and its trigger-phrase examples are `aria-hidden` and excluded from the crawlable DOM — answer engines receive only the safe reader-language descriptions. Reduced-motion renders every asset in complete final state with zero animation.

**Performance.** Inline SVG keeps every illustration light, themeable, and animatable without raster payload; grain textures and photography lazy-load and are omitted under reduced-data; the dark band and full illustrations defer until near-viewport. Motion is rationed to three landmark draw-on-scroll beats — the rest fade or stay static — protecting both attention and the frame budget. All entrance animation is `stroke-dashoffset`/opacity (GPU-light), capped ≤700ms, never looping. Reserved aspect-ratio boxes eliminate layout shift; text is real HTML so it renders before any illustration; no asset blocks first paint. Optional hero video is progressive enhancement only, never loaded on mobile, with a poster fallback always present.
