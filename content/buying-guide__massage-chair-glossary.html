Short answer

This massage chair glossary defines 45 terms from spec sheets, showroom floors and warranty pages. Each definition is brand-neutral. When a brand uses its own label, compare what the feature does, not what it is called.

Numbers: 2D to 5D rollers

2D rollers
Massage rollers that move up, down and side to side at a fixed depth.
3D rollers
Rollers that also extend forward from the backrest, so you can adjust how deeply they press.
4D rollers
3D rollers with speed or rhythm control. The 4D massage chair label is not standardized; see tracks and rollers explained.
5D rollers
A marketing label with no shared definition. Ask what the extra “dimension” does.

A

Airbag compression
Air cells in the shoulders, arms, seat, calves or feet that squeeze and release.
Arm airbags
Air cells in the armrests that compress the forearms and hands. Check that your arms fit.
Authorized dealer
A seller the manufacturer recognizes. Several warranties exclude chairs bought elsewhere, so confirm this in writing.
Auto programs
Preset routines that combine rollers, airbags, heat and recline. Run the same one on each chair you compare.

B

Body scan
Body scan technology senses your shoulder height and adjusts the roller range. It cannot fit a body outside the stated height range.

C

Calf kneading
Rollers in the leg rest that work the calves, often with airbags. The calf wells should not pinch.
Curbside delivery
Delivery that ends at the curb. You move the box inside and assemble the chair.

D

Dual mechanism
Two roller mechanisms, usually one for the upper back and one for the lower body, that can run at once.

E

Extended warranty (service contract)
Optional coverage on top of the standard warranty. The FTC’s guide to extended warranties and service contracts suggests checking who backs it and any fees.

F

Floor model
A chair that has been on display. Some brands give floor units a shorter or prorated warranty.
Foot rollers
Rollers in the soles of the foot wells. Many chairs let you turn them down or off.
Footrest extension
A leg rest that slides out or retracts so your feet stay in the foot wells.
Frame warranty
Coverage for the structural frame, often longer than parts or labor coverage.

H

Heat
Heating elements, usually in the lumbar area. Often listed as heat therapy; check how warm it gets and whether it switches off.
HSA and FSA
A health savings account (HSA) and a flexible spending account (FSA) are tax-advantaged accounts some people use for qualified medical expenses. Whether any purchase qualifies depends on IRS rules and your plan administrator; this glossary makes no eligibility claim. The IRS explains medical expenses in Publication 502.

I

In-home service
Repairs by a technician at your home. Warranties often limit it by distance; see in-home service in our warranty guide.

K

Kneading
A circular, gripping roller motion that imitates hands kneading muscle.

L

L-track
A roller track that runs down the back and on under the seat, forming an L shape. See the tracks and rollers guide.
Labor warranty
Coverage for a technician’s time, often shorter than parts coverage.
LMN (letter of medical necessity)
A letter from a licensed healthcare provider that some plan administrators request before reimbursing certain purchases. Requirements vary by plan, so ask your administrator directly.

M

Massage mechanism
The motorized carriage that moves the rollers along the track and sets the roller dimension.

N

Non-transferable warranty
Coverage for the original purchaser only. It may not carry over to a used chair.

P

Parts warranty
Coverage for components such as motors, airbags, rollers and circuit boards.
Proof of purchase
A dated receipt or invoice. Most manufacturer warranties require it for a claim.

R

Recline angle
How far the backrest tilts back. A deeper recline needs more floor and wall space.
Restocking fee
A charge deducted from a refund on a returned chair; see returns in our warranty guide.
Return window
The days you have to return a chair. Confirm when it starts and who pays return freight.
Rolling
A smooth up-and-down roller stroke along the spine.

S

S-track
A curved track that follows the spine from neck to lower back. S-track vs L-track: an S-track stops at the base of the backrest, while an L-track continues under the seat.
SL-track
An S-curve along the back plus an L-extension under the seat. Brands use variant names.
Shiatsu
A pressing technique inspired by Japanese finger-pressure massage. On a spec sheet, shiatsu massage names a roller pattern.
Stretch
A program that holds the legs with airbags while the backrest moves. Try it first.
Surge protector
A power strip that absorbs voltage spikes. Some warranties require one, such as 2,000 joules or more.

T

Tapping
A rapid percussive roller motion with adjustable speed.
Threshold delivery
Delivery just inside your door. Unpacking and assembly are usually up to you.
Track length
How far the rollers can travel, in inches or centimeters. Brands measure it differently, so compare it with your torso.

U

User height range
The heights a chair is designed for, such as 5′0″ to 6′2″. Outside it, rollers may miss your shoulders.

W

Wall-hugger (space-saving design)
A chair that slides forward as it reclines, so it needs less space behind it.
Weight capacity
The maximum user weight the manufacturer states, such as 265 lb or 300 lb.
White-glove delivery
Delivery to your chosen room with assembly and packaging removal. Confirm what is included.

Z

Zero gravity
A reclined position that raises the legs to around heart level and spreads your weight across the seat and backrest.

A note on wellness terms

Technique names such as shiatsu, kneading or stretch describe how a chair moves, not what it does for a medical condition. A massage chair is a comfort and relaxation product; if you have a diagnosed condition, talk with a qualified healthcare professional before using one. See our guide to reading massage chair claims.
